# Spec notes

Behaviour inferred beyond the reference manual, with reasoning.

- **N1. Internal clock curve.** Period = 16 s × (1/1280)^Time, so Time 0 is 16 s and Time 1 is 1/80 s (12.5 ms), exponential between. Equal knob travel gives equal ratios, which is how a rate knob feels.
- **N2. Host-sync steps.** Time is split into 9 equal zones, slowest first: /16, /8, /4, /2, ×1, ×2, ×3, ×4, ×8 (ticks per beat 1/16 … 8), so the knob's direction matches Internal (clockwise = faster). /16 = one tick every 16 beats. Ticks sit on the host's beat grid (tick k at beat k / rate), so they're phase-locked to bar position, and a transport start or loop jump lands straight back on the grid. With the transport stopped, the clock free-runs at the host tempo and the same step.
- **N3. Window length.** The most recent `period` samples, rounded to a whole sample (capped at what the buffer holds), captured *before* the tick's own sample is recorded. So at Repeats 1 and speed 1 the output is the input exactly one period late, bit for bit.
- **N4. Minimum fade.** Whenever windowing is above 0, a slice-edge fade is at least 1 ms (or half the slice, if that's shorter). On short slices, 2% of a slice is a few samples, which is itself a click. At 0 the edges stay hard.
- **N5. Mid-slice jumps.** When a tick (or Reset) arrives while the current slice is still audible, the old read head fades out over the new slice's fade length (32 samples to 20 ms) instead of being cut. At a slice end its envelope is already down, so nothing is needed.
- **N6. Repeats changes** take effect at the next slice boundary or tick, never mid-slice.
- **N7. Width.** 0–100% maps the wet signal's side to ×0 … ×2. 50% is as recorded (the default), 0 is mono, 100% doubles the side.
- **N8. Freeze modes.** Latching (the default) engages and releases on the next clock tick; Momentary acts at once. Freezing at Mix 0 forces fully wet until release (§3.1).
- **N9. Micro Bend.** Speed = 2^(−3 + 6·Bend) octaves: the knob's centre is 1×, the ends ±3 octaves, smoothed over 20 ms in octaves. "On an exact octave" means within ±5 cents.
- **N10. Note to Speed.** Reference C3 = MIDI 60 = 1× (Live's numbering), one octave per 12 semitones, added to Bend. Last note wins, and the pitch holds after note-off, like a CV that stays where it was left. Note changes jump at once, so they're in tune from the first sample. Gate notes (C1–F1 by default) never set the pitch.
- **N11. Micro Break.** `breakOnMicro` off = Traverse, on = Silence.
  - **Traverse:** slice = floor(Break × Repeats). A change jumps there at once, keeping the position within the slice like a CD skipping, with a fade-out tail and a fade-in.
  - **Silence:** the last 90% × Break of each slice's playback is silent, with windowing fades at its edge.
- **N12. Reverse** starts each new slice at its end. A direction change fades like a jump.
- **N13. The slice envelope follows the head's position** in the slice, in playback direction, not the time since the slice began. With a speed change mid-slice (a new note), a time-based envelope fell silent until the next wrap. A position-based one stays right, and mid-slice jumps add a separate fade-in.
- **N14. Macro zones.** Both knobs run through 7 cumulative zones; each adds an option. Bend: off, reverse, octave (±1), two octaves (±2), slew, tape stop, everything. Break: off, halve, jump (to any slice), subdivide (extra repeats on top of the knob's), audio-rate repeats, silence, everything. The chance per tick rises linearly through each zone, and once something happens the option is picked by per-zone weights, so a zone's own option dominates while earlier ones stay possible. Zone boundaries start evenly spaced with a short Off at the bottom and a wider Everything at the top. All of it is in `MacroTables` for tuning.
- **N15. Macro Bend events last one tick.** A tick with no event plays forward at 1×. Pitch, slew and stop events may also play reversed (a per-zone chance). Slew glides from wherever the speed is to a random target within ±2 octaves, with a time constant of 10–60% of the period. Tape stop winds from 1× to a standstill over 25–90% of the period, as (1 − t)^1.4, and fades out below 4% speed so the standstill is silent rather than DC; the next tick restarts it. In the Everything zone, a hard direction change adds a short vinyl-style click.
- **N16. Macro Break shapes the window.** Halve loops half a slice (first or second). Subdivide cuts each slice into 3–16 pieces (synced clocks favour 4, 8 and 16), usually looping the first piece (a ratchet). Audio rate cuts slices into pieces repeating at 40–320 Hz (log-uniform), usually on the transient. Jump plays a random slice of the cut window. Silence mutes 20–90% of each repeat. In Everything, cut, jump and silence can stack (45% each).
- **N17. Break depends on Time, Repeats and clock.** Options that can't work are skipped: jump needs Repeats > 1, halve and subdivide never make pieces shorter than 8 ms, and audio rate needs a slice long enough for two cycles. Ticks faster than 0.25 s get proportionally fewer events (chance × √(period / 0.25 s)), for Bend too, so fast clocks don't become wall-to-wall noise.
- **N18. Unique and Shared stereo.** Each channel has its own read head. Unique: each rolls its own dice every tick. Shared: one roll for both, so a mono source stays mono.
- **N19. Seed.** 0 (the default) draws a new seed per session, and the dice never repeat. Any other seed makes each tick's dice a function of (seed, tick index), where the index is the host grid position when synced. A given bar then always glitches the same way, wherever playback starts or loops.
- **N20. Mode switches reset Macro state.** Switching to Micro drops any Macro speed, cut or silence at once. Switching back starts at 1× with no cuts until the next tick.
- **N21. Corrupt sits on the wet only,** after the slice envelope and silence and before Width, as in the brief's signal flow. At Mix 0 it's inaudible.
- **N22. Corrupt knob 0 is off, except for DJ Filter,** whose centre is off (a ±2% dead zone) and whose left and right ends are the deepest low-pass and high-pass. Off means bypassed, with no processing and output bit-identical to Corrupt not existing.
- **N23. Decimate presets** are one per twelfth of the knob, stepped, not blended, crossfading over 30 ms at each step. The order is deliberately shuffled (bit depth and rate don't move together) but trends heavier: 12-bit with hiss, then 10-bit, 14-bit at 11 kHz, 8-bit, … down to 2-bit at 1.5 kHz with jitter. Rates are in Hz, so a preset sounds the same at any host rate. No anti-aliasing: the aliasing is the sound.
- **N24. Dropout** is a random (Poisson) process. Its rate rises from 0.25 to 16 a second across the knob while the mean length falls from 1 s to 12 ms, so it mutes about a fifth of the time throughout. Both channels drop together, with 3 ms fades. Moving the knob changes the next dropout, never the current one.
- **N25. Destroy** saturates with tanh up to +30 dB of drive at halfway. The second half hardens into clipping (to +42 dB) and blends in a sine wavefolder. A 10 Hz DC blocker follows the shaper (added 19 Nov 2026, found by the capability survey): saturating an asymmetric signal, such as a voice, shifts its mean, which reached about −24 dBFS of DC at full drive. Level: a linked RMS match over 150 ms that only turns down, then a zero-latency limiter and a clamp at −1 dBFS, so full-scale input at full drive never goes over and never gets louder.
- **N26. DJ Filter** is a TPT state-variable filter with Q 0.9 rising to 1.7 towards the ends. The low-pass runs 20 kHz → 50 Hz and the high-pass 20 Hz → 8 kHz, both exponential. It blends in from dry over the first 8% of each side.
- **N27. Vinyl** rises together across the knob: crackle (3 → 600 impulses a second, per channel, through a 2.5 kHz band-pass), pops (rare, low, shared), hiss, an EQ narrowing to 170 Hz – 3.3 kHz, and wow (0.55 → 1.3 Hz, up to ±1%) with a little 7 Hz flutter. The noise goes in before the EQ, as if it came off the stylus. Wow is a modulated delay of at most ~3 ms on the wet.
- **N28. Corrupt Set "Original 3":** DJ Filter or Vinyl selected while the set is Original 3 plays Decimate. The Corrupt gate (Stage 5) steps through the set and wraps.
- **N29. Corrupt follows the seed.** Dropout, Vinyl and Decimate's jitter and dither take their randomness from the seed: the session seed when the seed is 0, otherwise a fixed one. Renders stay bit-identical for the same seed.
- **N30. Modulation is an offset on the knob's 0–1 travel,** clamped. Repeats uses its exponential knob (32^x), so ±depth means the same travel at any count. Time on the host clock steps through the div/mult list. Several routes into one destination add up.
- **N31. Control rate.** Modulators, CC smoothing and the offsets update every 32 samples of absolute output time, so results don't depend on host block sizes. The envelope follower runs every sample. Synced modulators with the host playing are a function of the beat position, re-read exactly at each clock tick, so a synced S&H and the ticks on the same grid always agree (a new Repeats on every 1/16 tick, not one step late).
- **N32. Modulator shapes.** Sine and triangle start at 0 going up, the saw rises, and the square is high for the first half. S&H holds one random value per cycle; smooth random glides between them (cosine). Random values are a function of (seed, modulator, cycle), so with a fixed seed and the host clock a given bar always gets the same values. A bar is taken as 4 beats. Synced and stopped, they free-run at the host tempo.
- **N33. Envelope follower:** peak of both channels of the input, attack 0.1–500 ms, release 1–5000 ms, gain ±24 dB, clamped to 0–1 (always unipolar).
- **N34. CC lanes.** Defaults are CC 1 (mod wheel), 16, 17, 18 (general purpose 1–3). Values are smoothed over 10 ms. Unipolar is 0–1; bipolar maps CC 0–127 to −1…+1, so 64 is close to the centre.
- **N35. Gates.** Latching flips the toggle on each note-on; momentary flips it while the note is held. A flip is applied on top of the parameter (XOR), so a gate also works the other way round when the parameter is already on. Bend and Break gates act on the current mode's toggle. The Corrupt gate steps to the next type in the set, and in Momentary steps back on release. Freeze's timing follows Freeze Button: latching engages on the next tick, momentary at once. Changing Gate Mode clears every flip, so nothing sticks.
- **N36. Sample accuracy.** The engine splits each block at every MIDI event, so a gate, reset or clock pulse acts on its own sample. A Reset on the internal clock ticks on that sample; a change that crossfades starts its fade there.
- **N37. Note clock.** Each note-on of the Note clock gate note is a pulse, and the interval since the previous pulse is measured. ×N: a tick on the pulse, then N − 1 more evenly spaced. /N: a tick on every Nth pulse. Before the second pulse the interval is assumed to be one beat at the host tempo. With no pulse for four intervals it carries on at the last rate, on the same grid, until pulses return.
- **N38. Reset.** Internal: a new window at once. Host, playing: the division realigns so the next beat starts it, which matters for the slow divisions. Note: the division counter realigns so the next pulse ticks. A transport start (from a known stop) is a Reset too, and puts the host grid back on the bar. The Reset parameter acts at the start of its block; the gate is sample-accurate.
- **N39. Buffer view, ghost.** The view grows with the window at once and shrinks towards it with a 0.9 s time constant, so for a moment after Time shrinks the older audio is still in view (dimmed, left of the window start). Very short windows are drawn from 32-sample buckets, so below about 0.3 s the waveform is a little blocky.
- **N40. Drag-to-assign** reuses a route that already joins the source to that knob, else takes the first empty slot (Source or Dest None) at 50% depth. With all 8 in use it says so and changes nothing.
- **N41. Momentary gates count their notes (Stage 7).** A momentary gate is held while any of its note-ons is still down. The rules:
  - A doubled note-on steps Corrupt once, not twice.
  - A note-off with nothing held is ignored.
  - Bend and Break remember which mode's toggle they flipped, so switching mode mid-hold still lets the right one go.
  - All Notes Off (CC 123) and All Sound Off (CC 120) release every held gate.
  - A gate whose note number changes while it's held (by learning) is released.

  Latching is unchanged: every note-on flips.
- **N42. Hot inputs.** Without the output clipper (N43), nothing limits the output except Destroy. A full-scale square wave through the DJ Filter's high-pass side, or Vinyl's rumble filter, overshoots by up to about 9 dB. Width at 100% on unrelated left and right channels adds up to 6 dB more. The worst case found is +14 dBFS from a 0 dBFS input, and the fuzz never exceeded +24 dBFS before the Output trim (N44), which can add up to 12 dB more on purpose. On real material the factory presets peak at or below about +0.1 dBFS.
- **N43. Output clip** (Settings, on by default) is the last thing on the output, dry included. Below −1 dBFS it does nothing. From there to 0 dBFS a tanh knee joins the straight line with no corner, and nothing ever passes 0 dBFS. It's stateless with no latency and adds no oversampling, so it's a safety net, not a sound. The buffer view shows "Clip" for half a second whenever it acts. Turn it off for float headroom (for example, when a limiter follows). Older sessions and presets load with it on. Restore defaults turns it back on.
- **N44. Output trim** (Settings, −24 to +12 dB, default 0) scales dry and wet together, just before the output clip, so the clip still has the last word. It glides over 20 ms. At 0 dB with nothing gliding it's skipped entirely, so the output stays bit-exact. It exists so presets can arrive at a matched loudness: repeating loud slices (drum hits) raised the old presets by up to 6 dB. Restore defaults sets it to 0 dB. State version 8; older sessions load at 0 dB.
- **N45. Very short repeated slices carry DC.** Looping a few milliseconds of a bass waveform repeats that slice's mean, which isn't zero: up to about −29 dBFS of DC at Repeats 16 on the ×4 clock in the survey's mix. It's inaudible and the slice changes every tick, so it's left alone: a DC blocker on the wet would break N3 (Repeats 1 is the input, exactly one period late).
- **N46. Break this bar** (George, 20 Nov 2026: a "break this bar" button, plus an auto mode). A fill waits for its place, plays glitched, and lets go exactly on the downbeat.
  - **Where it lands:** the first place that ends on a bar line and starts no earlier than now. A 1-bar or 2-bar fill starts on the next bar line. A 1-beat or 2-beat fill is the end of the bar, leading into the downbeat. Up to a 1/16 note (0.25 beat) late still counts: pressed just after a downbeat, that bar breaks, starting at once. With the transport stopped it starts at once and lasts its length at the host tempo (120 if none).
  - **What it does:** fully wet, Macro mode, on the host-synced clock at 1/8 notes below 40% Amount and 1/16 from there, Repeats 1. The Break dice always play (knob at least 20% + 70% × Amount). The Bend dice play from 25% (at least 10% + 75% × Amount). Above 75%, Corrupt creeps in (up to 50%, the current type). Knobs already further up keep their own setting. A fixed Seed makes fills repeatable.
  - **Clean on the downbeat:** the clock ticks on the fill's first sample and again on the downbeat after it, whatever the clock source, so the next window is fresh audio. Mix glides back over its usual 20 ms.
  - **Triggers:** the button (momentary, like Reset), the `fillTrigger` parameter's rising edge (automation or a key/MIDI mapping in the host), or the Break bar gate note. That gate has no note until you learn one (a default would steal a pitch from existing sets). Gate mode doesn't apply to it: it's a one-shot. A press while a fill plays is ignored, and a press while armed re-aims it.
  - **Auto (Every 2/4/8/16 bars):** the fill ends on each bar line whose bar number is a multiple of N, counted from the song start, so 4 means bars 4, 8, 12 and so on. Each one plays with Chance. With a fixed Seed the dice are keyed on the bar, so the same bars break on every pass.
  - **Bars** come from the host's time signature and last bar position (4/4 from 0 if the host gives none). A loop, a locate or a transport start/stop drops a fill that's armed or playing, and goes back to clean.
  - **State version 9:** five new parameters (Break This Bar, Break Length, Break Amount, Break Every, Break Chance), added last so existing automation doesn't move, plus a seventh gate note. Older sessions load with the fill by hand, 1 bar, 60%, and no gate note. Restore defaults sets Every back to By hand.
